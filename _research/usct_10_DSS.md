### Overview & Clinical Motivation

Reflection ultrasound computed tomography (RUCT) using ring-array or circular apertures provides high-resolution, full-aperture cross-sectional imaging of acoustic reflectivity. However, high-frame-rate acquisition and computational scalability necessitate **sparse transmit schemes** (reducing the number of active transmit events $N_{Tx}$).

Severe transmit undersampling introduces prominent acoustic streak artifacts, grating lobes, and diffraction clutter that compromise target detectability, degrade contrast-to-noise ratio (CNR), and obscure tissue interfaces. 

To overcome these sparse-sampling degradations without modifying acquisition hardware, we propose a **Data-Subset Stability (DSS) framework for Reflection USCT**. By partitioning the total transmit sequence into independent subsets, DSS exploits the geometric invariance of true acoustic reflectors against the fluctuating nature of undersampling artifacts.

### Key Technical Details & Methodology

- **Transmit Subset Partitioning:**
  - In a sparse RUCT acquisition with sparsity factors ranging from $1$ to $16$, the available transmit events are partitioned into $M$ disjoint, interleaved transmit subsets:
    $$
    \mathcal{T} = \bigcup_{m=1}^M \mathcal{T}_m, \quad \text{where } \mathcal{T}_i \cap \mathcal{T}_j = \emptyset \text{ for } i \neq j.
    $$

- **Independent Subset SAFT Reconstruction:**
  - For each transmit subset $\mathcal{T}_m$, a sub-aperture Synthetic Aperture Focusing Technique (SAFT) image $I_m(\mathbf{r})$ is reconstructed:
    $$
    I_m(\mathbf{r}) = \sum_{e_{Tx} \in \mathcal{T}_m} \sum_{e_{Rx}} s\left(e_{Tx}, e_{Rx}, \tau(\mathbf{r}, e_{Tx}, e_{Rx})\right),
    $$
    where $\tau(\mathbf{r}, e_{Tx}, e_{Rx})$ represents the round-trip propagation delay from transmit element $e_{Tx}$ to pixel $\mathbf{r}$ and back to receive element $e_{Rx}$.

- **Pixel-Wise Stability Estimation:**
  - Because true acoustic scatterers possess consistent phase and amplitude profiles across varying transmit angles, their pixel intensities remain stable across $\{I_m(\mathbf{r})\}_{m=1}^M$. In contrast, undersampling artifacts and grating lobes shift dynamically across subsets.
  - Inter-subset consistency is evaluated by calculating the pixel-wise mean $\mu_I(\mathbf{r})$ and standard deviation $\sigma_I(\mathbf{r})$ across subsets to generate a stability metric:
    $$
    \mathrm{DSS}(\mathbf{r}) = \frac{\mu_I(\mathbf{r})}{\sigma_I(\mathbf{r}) + \epsilon},
    $$
    where $\epsilon > 0$ ensures numerical stability in homogeneous background regions.

- **Stability-Guided Composite Beamforming:**
  - The estimated stability map is normalized and transformed into a spatial weighting function $W_{\mathrm{DSS}}(\mathbf{r}) \in [0, 1]$.
  - The final reconstructed image is generated by weighting the full-aperture composite SAFT image $I_{\mathrm{SAFT}}(\mathbf{r})$:
    $$
    I_{\mathrm{final}}(\mathbf{r}) = W_{\mathrm{DSS}}(\mathbf{r}) \cdot I_{\mathrm{SAFT}}(\mathbf{r}).
    $$
  - Real reflectors and anatomical boundaries are reinforced, whereas incoherent transmit-undersampling artifacts are strongly attenuated.

### Experimental Validation & Quantitative Results

The framework was systematically validated across transmit sparsity factors ($1\times$ to $16\times$) using numerical, experimental phantom, and dynamic *in vivo* datasets:

1. **Point-Source Phantom:** Evaluated point-spread function (PSF) distortion and side-lobe suppression under severe angular sparsity.
2. **Three-Circle Phantom:** Benchmarked contrast preservation, resolution retention, and background clutter clearance.
3. **In-Vivo Mouse Model:** Validated cross-sectional anatomical fidelity, tissue boundary definition, and clutter suppression in complex biological media.

| Metric / Scenario | Conventional SAFT | Proposed DSS-RUCT | Quantitative Gain |
|:---|:---:|:---:|:---:|
| **Phantom SNR Gain** | Baseline | Enhanced | **Up to +19 dB** |
| **Phantom Contrast (CNR)** | Degraded at $16\times$ sparsity | High Contrast Retained | **Substantial streak clearance** |
| **In-Vivo Background Fluctuations** | High acoustic clutter | Clean background | **~5 dB Reduction** |
| **Boundary Delineation** | Blurred interfaces | Crisp tissue boundaries | **Significantly Improved** |

### Key Innovations & Impact

- **Physics-Guided Transmit Stability:**  
  Establishes a stability-criterion framework based purely on transmit-event partitioning, effectively separating physical reflections from geometric sampling artifacts.

- **Severe Sparsity Tolerance (Up to $16\times$):**  
  Maintains high-fidelity image contrast and resolution even when up to 93.75% of transmit events are omitted, enabling ultra-fast frame rates.

- **Non-Iterative & Hardware-Agnostic:**  
  Operates strictly as a post-processing reconstruction filter without requiring computationally intensive inverse solvers, deep neural network training, or modified pulse-echo sequence hardware.

- **Speckle and Boundary Preservation:**  
  Avoids the severe structural over-smoothing common to non-linear beamformers, ensuring crisp visualization of organ margins and fine acoustic interfaces.

- **Plug-and-Play Integration:**  
  Directly compatible with existing circular-array reflection USCT systems, clinical ring transducers, and real-time GPU beamforming pipelines.


- **Broad Validation:**  
  Demonstrated using simulated, experimental, and in vivo datasets, including breast, leaf, triangle, and rat-brain phantoms.

- **Potential for Efficient PACT:**  
  The framework may enable lower-channel-count and reduced-acquisition PACT systems while preserving image quality and structural reliability.
