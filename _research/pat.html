Featured Ring-Array PACT Image Reconstruction

Full-Ring Ultrasonic Array PACT for Deep-Tissue Preclinical Imaging

Development of a high-channel circular transducer system with optimized acoustic aperture synthesis.

Fig 1. Custom 512-element ring-array transducer and pulsed laser illumination setup.

Technical Overview & Methodology

In this project, we designed and integrated a custom multi-channel ring-array acquisition architecture tailored for fast whole-body small-animal tomography. By coupling 532 nm and 1064 nm Q-switched Nd:YAG laser pulses with panoramic detection geometry, the system circumvents limited-view acoustic distortion and isotropic in-plane resolution.

Our reconstruction pipeline integrates model-based iterative reconstruction (MBIR) incorporating spatio-temporal transducer impulse response (EIR/SIR) calibration, improving signal-to-noise ratio (SNR) by over 12 dB in deep vascular beds compared to standard Delay-and-Sum (DAS).

Acoustic Speed-of-Sound (SoS) Aberration Correction in PACT

Dual-modal USCT/PACT mapping to eliminate acoustic refraction and phase distortion in heterogeneous tissue.

Fig 2. Comparison between homogeneous assumption vs. full-wave SoS corrected reconstruction.

Technical Overview & Methodology

Assuming a constant speed of sound ($c_0 = 1540\text{ m/s}$) produces significant geometric distortion, blurring, and split-vessel artifacts in organs with high acoustic contrast such as breast margins and skull boundaries.

We established an interleaved transmission-reflection ultrasound tomography (USCT) sequence that extracts spatially resolved sound-speed distributions. These maps feed directly into a generalized time-reversal forward acoustic solver, restoring sub-millimeter edge definitions.

Deep Learning Sparse Sampling

Sparse-View PACT Reconstruction via Physics-Informed Neural Networks

Accelerating acquisition speed and lowering sensor channel costs using learned deep priors.

Fig 3. Artifact mitigation from 32-element sparse acquisitions using deep residual UNet.

Technical Overview & Methodology

To reduce the high hardware costs of thousands of DAQ channels, sparse-array sampling is required. However, spatial undersampling introduces severe streak artifacts and grating lobes that obscure microvascular networks.

We implemented an unrolled physics-guided deep neural network combining iterative gradient descent with deep regularization steps, enabling 8× channel sub-sampling without sacrificing quantitative optical absorption metrics.
