Abstract
Streak artifacts arising from sparse detector-sampling conditions remain a major challenge in circular photoacoustic computed tomography (PACT), often obscuring real absorbers or causing their intensities to be underestimated. Existing artifact-suppression methods often reduce streak artifacts at the expense of attenuating true absorber signals, leading to partial or missing structural recovery. In this work, we introduce a Detector-Subset Stability (DSS)-guided artifact suppression framework that suppresses artifacts while preserving absorber morphology and intensity. The proposed method generates multiple detector-subset images (DSIs), computes a pixel-wise detector-subset stability ratio to identify artifact-prone regions, and applies the resulting stability map to the full-detector image (FDI) without distorting true absorber signals. Simulation studies on a breast phantom, together with experiments on leaf and triangle phantoms and in vivo rat brain data, demonstrate that the proposed approach effectively suppresses streak artifacts while preserving absorber morphology and intensity close to the ground truth. Quantitative evaluation further demonstrates consistently superior performance, achieving the highest Precision and F1 scores (96–98%), improved generalized contrast-to-noise ratio (gCNR = 0.67–0.73), and signal-to-noise ratio (SNR) gains of up to 57 dB under severe detector undersampling. These results demonstrate that the proposed DSS-guided framework provides a simple, non-iterative solution for suppressing streak artifacts while maintaining high structural fidelity and accurate absorber intensity in circular PACT.
