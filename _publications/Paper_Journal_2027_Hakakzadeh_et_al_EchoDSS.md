Abstract
Acoustic clutter and reverberation degrade image quality and obscure myocardial boundaries in echocardiography. While coherence-based beamformers improve cavity contrast, they often distort speckle texture or cause over-smoothing. We propose Stability-Guided Pixel-Wise Correlation (SG-PWC) beamforming, an adaptive method that evaluates the statistical dispersion of normalized channel cross-correlations across multiple short spatial lags. Stable, coherent tissue echoes are preserved, while unstable clutter is penalized and suppressed. Evaluated on in-vivo cardiac data (PLAX and PSAX views), SG-PWC achieves superior contrast performance with a gCNR of 0.98 and CNR of 4.2 (outperforming SLSC at 0.99/3.2, and DAS/DMAS at 0.96/2.5) while maintaining natural speckle fidelity.
