### Overview & Clinical Motivation

Cardiac ultrasound is routinely used to assess ventricular morphology, myocardial motion, chamber dimensions, and cardiac function. However, echocardiographic images are frequently degraded by acoustic clutter, reverberation, and incoherent echoes. These artifacts can obscure the ventricular cavity and reduce the visibility of myocardial and endocardial boundaries.

Conventional **Delay-and-Sum (DAS)** beamforming preserves natural speckle texture but provides limited clutter suppression. Nonlinear and coherence-based methods, including **Delay-Multiply-and-Sum (DMAS)** and **Short-Lag Spatial Coherence (SLSC)**, can improve cavity contrast but may distort speckle statistics or excessively smooth anatomical structures.

This work introduces **Stability-Guided Pixel-Wise Correlation (SG-PWC) beamforming**, also referred to as stability-guided correlation beamforming, which uses the consistency of spatial correlations across receive-channel lags to distinguish reliable coherent echoes from unstable clutter components.

### Key Technical Details & Methodology

- **Delayed Receive-Channel Analysis:**
  - For each image pixel $\mathbf{r}$, the delayed receive-channel signals are retained after geometric focusing:
    $$
    \mathbf{s}(\mathbf{r}) =
    \left[
    s_1(\mathbf{r}),
    s_2(\mathbf{r}),
    \ldots,
    s_N(\mathbf{r})
    \right]^T.
    $$

- **Short-Lag Normalized Correlation:**
  - Normalized cross-correlations are computed between receive channels separated by multiple short spatial lags:
    $$
    \rho_{\ell}(\mathbf{r}) =
    \frac{
    \left\langle s_i(\mathbf{r}),s_{i+\ell}(\mathbf{r})\right\rangle
    }{
    \|s_i(\mathbf{r})\|_2
    \|s_{i+\ell}(\mathbf{r})\|_2
    },
    $$
    where $\ell$ denotes the receive-channel separation and the correlations are evaluated over a set of selected short lags.



- **Correlation-Stability Estimation:**
  - Rather than using only the average correlation magnitude, SG-PWC analyzes the dispersion of lag-wise correlations around their mean:
    $$
    \bar{\rho}(\mathbf{r}) =
    \frac{1}{L}
    \sum_{\ell=1}^{L}\rho_{\ell}(\mathbf{r}),
    $$
    $$
    \sigma_{\rho}^{2}(\mathbf{r}) =
    \frac{1}{L}
    \sum_{\ell=1}^{L}
    \left[
    \rho_{\ell}(\mathbf{r})-\bar{\rho}(\mathbf{r})
    \right]^2.
    $$

- **Stability-Guided Weighting:**
  - A pixel-wise stability factor is generated by penalizing large deviations from the mean correlation:
    $$
    W_{\mathrm{SG}}(\mathbf{r}) =
    f\!\left(
    \bar{\rho}(\mathbf{r}),
    \sigma_{\rho}^{2}(\mathbf{r})
    \right),
    $$
    where the weighting function assigns larger values to pixels exhibiting consistent correlation across spatial lags.

- **Pixel-Wise Beamforming:**
  - The stability factor is applied to the conventional beamformed signal:
    $$
    I_{\mathrm{SG\text{-}PWC}}(\mathbf{r}) =
    W_{\mathrm{SG}}(\mathbf{r})
    I_{\mathrm{DAS}}(\mathbf{r}).
    $$
  - Coherent myocardial and endocardial echoes are reinforced, whereas unstable signal contributions associated with clutter and reverberation are attenuated.

- **In-Vivo Echocardiographic Evaluation:**
  - The method was evaluated using in-vivo cardiac ultrasound data acquired in:
    - Parasternal short-axis (**PSAX**) views.
    - Parasternal long-axis (**PLAX**) views.
  - Comparisons were performed against:
    - Conventional DAS.
    - DMAS.
    - SLSC.

### Quantitative Performance

| Beamformer | gCNR | CNR |
|:---|:---:|:---:|
| DAS | 0.96 | 2.5 |
| DMAS | 0.96 | 2.5 |
| SLSC | 0.99 | 3.2 |
| **SG-PWC** | **1.00** | **4.2** |

Compared with SLSC, SG-PWC improves CNR by approximately:

$$
\frac{4.2-3.2}{3.2}\times 100
\approx 31.3\%.
$$

The proposed method also achieves the highest reported gCNR, indicating strong separation between ventricular cavity and surrounding tissue distributions.

### Key Innovations & Impact

- **Stability-Based Coherence Assessment:**  
  Uses the dispersion of correlation values across spatial lags rather than relying solely on correlation magnitude.

- **Effective Cardiac Clutter Suppression:**  
  Reduces acoustic clutter and reverberation within ventricular cavities, producing cleaner chamber regions.

- **Improved Myocardial Boundary Delineation:**  
  Sharpens ventricular and myocardial interfaces while preserving coherent anatomical echoes.

- **Speckle-Preserving Enhancement:**  
  Compared with strongly smoothing coherence-based methods, SG-PWC maintains a more natural speckle appearance and structural texture.

- **Superior Contrast Performance:**  
  Achieves a gCNR of $1.00$ and a CNR of $4.2$, outperforming DAS, DMAS, and SLSC in the evaluated in-vivo cardiac data.

- **Computationally Adaptable Framework:**  
  The method operates on delayed receive-channel data and can potentially be implemented using parallel GPU or FPGA processing for real-time echocardiography.

- **Generalizability:**  
  The stability-guided weighting principle may also be applicable to carotid ultrasound, abdominal imaging, breast sonography, and other applications affected by spatially unstable clutter.
